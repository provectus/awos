## Honest reading — what the data does and does not say

1. **B > A fired at exactly the pre-registered minimum** (+2 passes out of 10). It counts — that is what pre-registration is for — but it is the smallest effect the rule accepts, at an n where two runs flipping would erase it. Treat it as "supports the proposal", not "proves it".
2. **The surprise is C < A (3 vs 6 passes).** The readout table anticipated B≈C ("information effect") or B>C ("notation effect"); it did not anticipate the information-matched prose arm underperforming the _baseline_. Two candidate explanations, undecidable at n=10: (a) noise — binomial spread at n=10 is wide; (b) the generated edge-list prose actively misleads — mechanically-derived sentences ("X (container, inside ShipLog) → Y…") may read as authoritative while being harder to integrate than either clean prose or a diagram. If (b) is real, it strengthens the notation claim (the _same information_ helped as a diagram and hurt as generated text), but it also means arm C's derivation format is itself a variable worth one design pass before phase 2.
3. **The hallucination branch of the rule never engaged** — arm A's mean stayed at zero, which is what the branch keys on, so the pass/fail metric carried the whole verdict. A phase-2 fixture wanting to exercise that metric needs stronger hallucination bait (more similarly-named services, plausible-but-absent integration points). _Correction 2026-10-16: a grader defect originally reported all arms as 0.0; the corrected re-grade shows small nonzero counts in arms B/C that are unrealized planned paths, not invented integrations — see `2026-10-16-c4-eval-grader-correction.md`. The verdict is unaffected._
4. **Secondary metrics cut both ways.** B showed less drift than A (5.3 vs 6.7) — consistent with the proposal's drift claim — but more out-of-scope edits than C (1.4 vs 0.5). None of these gated, and none were pre-registered as decisive.
5. **Standing limitations** (from the plan): one synthetic fixture, hand-authored best-case diagrams, Sonnet only, single-maintainer diagram sign-off (2026-10-12 amendment).

## How to read the cost — diagrams are a quality buy, not an efficiency win

To prevent a misreading that came up in review: the diagram arm used **3.5% _more_ tokens** than the baseline (4195 vs 4053 mean per run), not 3.5% _of_ the baseline's tokens. The overhead is mechanical — arm B's context documents are strictly longer (arm A plus the diagram sections), so every session reads more input. Wall-clock was essentially flat (33.4s vs 32.8s mean).

The pre-registered rule anticipated exactly this trade: a winning arm was allowed up to +15% token regression, and B came in at +3.5%. So the defensible one-line claim is:

> **+2 correct implementations out of 10, for +3.5% tokens and no meaningful wall-clock change.**

This campaign found **no** dimension where diagrams made runs cheaper or faster. The case for C4 diagrams is quality per token, not efficiency — anyone quoting this result should say so.

## Recommendation: proceed to phase 2 — yes

This result does not ship the template change as "proven", but it clears the bar for spending more on the question, for three reasons:

1. **The direction is right and the mechanism is the interesting one.** The strongest branch (`notation-effect`) fired: the diagram beat not just the baseline but the information-matched text arm. That is precisely the claim skeptics doubted, and it survived its first pre-registered test.
2. **The effect is at the threshold, so it needs replication — and replication is cheap.** +2/10 at n=10 flips with two runs. But a full campaign cost ~2 hours of subscription wall-clock and roughly 125K output tokens total; one more campaign is a trivial price against changing the framework's core templates on a coin-flip-adjacent result.
3. **Phase 1 exposed fixable instrument gaps** that phase 2 should close before the numbers are quoted upstream: the hallucination metric never engaged (the fixture needs real bait — more near-name collisions, plausible-but-absent integration points), and arm C's generated wording is a confound candidate (one design pass on the edge-list phrasing, or drop C and run A/B only at n=15 each).

**Concrete phase-2 scope (recommended):** a second synthetic fixture with hallucination bait and a harder boundary-crossing feature; n=10 per arm, A and B mandatory, C only after its wording review; same pre-registered rule. If B > A replicates, open the template/command PR per the proposal with both campaigns attached. If it does not replicate, the honest state is "one positive, one null at threshold" — iterate on the diagram convention (the proposal's own "mixed" branch) rather than shipping or abandoning.
